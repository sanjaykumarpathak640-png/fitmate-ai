Project: technical summary

FitMate AI by Team CodeCodders, PRASUNEX Innovathon 2026, Round 3 prototype. Full documentation and source code are in the project repository.

How it is built

  • Realistic 3D avatar (three.js + MakeHuman CC0 data): a 13,380-vertex human body with a real skin texture, eyes, brows and lashes. 143 morph targets shape it: build (gender, muscle, weight), face base, face shape, jaw, cheeks, nose, lips, eyes, ears and expression. A solver adjusts the bust, waist, hip and shoulder targets until the mesh measures your exact cm (tested within 0.5 cm). Arms are posed down, and the avatar blinks.
  • Avatar templates: 12 ready-made avatars, 6 face bases, 6 face shapes, 5 jaws, 4 cheeks, 6 noses, 6 lips, 6 eye shapes, 6 eye colours, 9 brows, 12 hair styles, 10 hair colours, 6 facial-hair styles, 10 skin tones, 6 body types, 4 glasses and 4 shoes. A simpler 3D body (and a 2D SVG avatar without WebGL) is the fallback.
  • 3D garment engine: every garment is draped on the realistic body from its own size-chart measurements: the body surface is cut by neckline, sleeve, hem and waistband fields, pushed out over a convex hull of each body slice scaled by the garment's ease (so cloth bridges hollows and hangs from the chest), relaxed with volume-keeping smoothing, and layered pants → top → jacket. Skirts, dresses and kurtas fall as a cone from the hips. Fabric textures, folds where loose, and a red tint and label where tight.
  • Catalog: 100 garments (50 boys, 50 girls), 28 garment types, 3 to 5 colours and six sizes each, generated from standard body charts plus each style’s designed ease and each brand’s offset.
  • Backend: Python FastAPI with AI try-on jobs (IDM-VTON via gradio_client), size and fit-report endpoints, and a test-results endpoint.
  • Testing: automated in-browser checks, a pytest suite that also checks the browser and Python engines give identical answers, and recorded user tests.

Size Engine rules

Ease = garment − body. Each size is scored by how far its ease falls outside the target range for your preferred fit (tight costs 1.5×), weighted per measurement; lowest score wins. Each garment shifts the range for its designed cut (oversized, skinny, flared) and stretch fabric allows negative ease.

Implemented features

Limitations

  • The avatar is realistic but not a scan of you: the face is built from templates, not from a selfie. Cloth is shaped geometrically, not physically simulated: it shows width, length and tight or loose areas, not real drape, stretch or fabric weight. Very large or very small bodies can look less natural.
  • The realistic avatar loads 8 MB of assets and needs the app served over http (a web host or the backend); opened as a plain file it falls back to the simpler 3D body.
  • The photo overlay is a flat image. Only AI try-on reshapes cloth on a real photo, and it needs the backend and a GPU Space (IDM-VTON, non-commercial licence).
  • The hosted demo page cannot call outside servers, so AI try-on runs when the frontend and backend run together.
  • Catalog, brands and size charts are sample data; measurements are entered by hand, not taken from photos.
  • Size thresholds are starting values that the user tests on the Testing page are meant to tune.

Future development

  • Supabase accounts, real retailer catalogs and an admin dashboard with CSV size-chart import.
  • Cloth physics (drape and stretch simulation) and poses.
  • GPU worker queue and result caching; a commercially licensed try-on model.
  • Selfie-to-face: fit the face templates to a photo, and body measurements estimated from two photos, as research.
  • Retailer Fit API widget and a browser extension.

Before you upload

Your photo is used only to preview clothes on you.

  • Overlay preview: the photo stays in this browser and is never uploaded.
  • AI try-on: the photo goes to the FitMate backend and the try-on model, and is deleted right after the image is generated.
  • “Delete my photo” removes it from this page at any time. Photos are never used to train models.
